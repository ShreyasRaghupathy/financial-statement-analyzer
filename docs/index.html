Automotive · FY24

Tata Motors vs. Mahindra & Mahindra

Ratio-based models (ROE, liquidity, solvency) on consolidated FY24 statements, with an operating leverage sensitivity test.

33.5%
Tata Motors ROE, FY24

Tata's higher return on equity came paired with materially higher earnings volatility. A 10% revenue decline was modeled through both cost structures to test how that volatility plays out under pressure.

  • Tata's higher operating leverage means a larger share of costs stay fixed as revenue falls — profit drops faster than revenue.
  • M&M's more diversified cost base cushions the same shock more effectively, despite a lower baseline ROE.
  • The trade-off: Tata's efficiency in good years is the same structure that hurts it in bad ones.

Semiconductors · FY25 / FY24

NVIDIA vs. AMD

Comparative margin analysis across a $130B+ and a $25B revenue base, stress-tested against a margin compression scenario.

$130B+
NVIDIA revenue base
$25B
AMD revenue base

Scale alone doesn't explain resilience — margin structure does. A 1,000 bps margin compression scenario was applied to both companies to see which profitability cushion actually holds.

  • At the tested compression, NVIDIA retains $71B+ in operating income — its margin base has room to absorb the hit.
  • AMD's thinner baseline margin means the same compression pushes it into negative operating income.
  • The scale gap between the two companies is large, but it's the margin gap that determines who survives the shock.

Banking · CAMELS framework

HDFC Bank vs. ICICI Bank

CAMELS-framework analysis with a credit risk stress test modeling a rise in loan-loss provisions.

-19.3%
HDFC earnings impact
-4.5%
ICICI earnings impact

A 50% rise in loan-loss provisions was modeled across both banks' FY financials to quantify provisioning resilience under the CAMELS framework.

  • ICICI's provisioning buffer absorbs the shock with a fraction of the earnings impact HDFC takes.
  • The gap highlights how differently two banks of similar scale can be positioned for a credit cycle downturn.
  • This is the same stress-testing methodology as the automotive and semiconductor studies, applied to a regulated-sector framework.

Open-source tool

The engine behind these three studies

The three case studies above were built by hand. This tool automates the same methodology — ratio calculation, peer-relative anomaly detection, and stress testing — so it can run on any set of companies, not just these three.

$ python3 financial_statement_analyzer.py companies.json \ --revenue-decline-pct 10 → computing liquidity, profitability, leverage, efficiency ratios → flagging peer-relative anomalies (z-score ≥ 1.0) → running stress scenario across all companies → generating analyst narrative
  • Computes the same four ratio families used across all three studies above: liquidity, profitability, leverage, efficiency.
  • Flags anomalies automatically by comparing each company against its peer set, rather than eyeballing the numbers.
  • Runs configurable stress scenarios — revenue decline, margin compression, provision shocks — on any input set.
  • Generates an analyst-style written summary of the findings as a final step.
